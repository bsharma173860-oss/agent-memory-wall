# Study plan: keep, offload or recompute? The energy of KV-cache decisions in LLM serving

Written on 5 November 2026, **before** the runs below (E3 sizes revised the same day, before any run, to fit the host's RAM), so the results can be judged against what we expected.
Private until the paper is out.

## Question

When an LLM serving engine has to drop, keep or offload the KV cache of a request that will come back (an agent
after a tool call, a chat after a pause), how much GPU and CPU energy does each choice cost, and how does that depend
on load, prompt length and pause length?

## What is already published (literature check, 5 Nov 2026)

- Energy benchmarks of serving: ML.ENERGY (arXiv 2505.06371), vLLM-only energy studies (2509.08867, 2607.09172),
  PrefixBench-H100 (2609.19657: 24-31% energy saved per request from prefix reuse, 40 requests, no load sweep).
- Request-level energy attribution (2608.00026, Shapley-based): done by others; not our contribution.
- Offload vs recompute for agents measured for latency only (CacheWise 2606.16824 and a Sept 2026 cs.LG paper).
- A position paper (2609.05565) calls for a transfer-versus-recompute energy model and does not evaluate one.
- No published vLLM vs SGLang joules-per-token comparison found.

## Hypotheses (decided before the runs)

- **H1 (engines).** Under both default and tuned settings, vLLM and SGLang use the same GPU energy per output token
  within 3%, while workload choices (batching, prefix reuse) change it by more than 5x.
- **H2 (re-prefill cost depends on load).** The energy penalty of a cache miss (miss / hit, J per output token)
  grows with load and prompt length: at most 1.5x at 1 user, at least 5x at 64 users, for prompts of 8k tokens or more.
- **H3 (offload under memory pressure).** For agent sessions that exceed GPU memory but fit GPU plus host memory
  (48 sessions of ~13k tokens, with an 80 GB host tier), host offload lowers GPU energy per turn by at least 20% and
  resume time-to-first-token p90 by at least 2x against GPU-only caching. With no pressure (16 sessions) the
  difference is within noise. Beyond both tiers (96 sessions) the benefit shrinks.
- **H4 (pauses).** Longer pauses (30 s vs 5 s) cause more eviction, so the offload benefit is larger at 30 s.
- **H5 (offload's own cost).** The extra CPU and host-memory energy of offloading is under 10% of the GPU energy
  it saves.

Each is reported as supported, not supported or unclear, with the numbers, whichever way it comes out.

## Added on 6 November 2026, during the run (before any admission or offload result)

Written after seeing round 1 of E3 for vLLM GPU-only and no-cache, and before any run of the policy below.

Seen so far: the GPU holds 244,928 tokens of KV (about 18 sessions). At 16 sessions the prefix cache hits 85% of
prompt tokens; at 48 and 96 sessions it hits 0.04% and 0.01%, and GPU-only uses the same energy per turn as no cache
at all (1,047 vs 1,064 J at 48 sessions, pause 5 s). The cache does not degrade with pressure, it collapses.

- **H6 (session admission).** Letting at most 16 sessions send turns (a session keeps its slot through its pauses and
  gives it up after 4 or 8 turns, FIFO; the engine is unchanged) raises the cache hit at 48 sessions from ~0 to at least
  30% and lowers GPU energy per turn by at least 25% against GPU-only, on both pause lengths. The time a session waits
  for a slot is counted in its resume latency and reported next to it.

- **H7 (eviction policy alone).** Seen before writing this: SGLang's default (LRU) radix cache collapses the same way
  (85% hit at 16 sessions, 0.12% at 48). Switching SGLang to LFU (`--radix-eviction-policy lfu`) keeps a stable subset
  of sessions and gets at least 20% cache hit at 48 sessions; SLRU (`slru`, default threshold) still collapses (under
  5%), because every session is reused often enough to enter the protected segment. Neither changes the 16-session
  result by more than 5% in energy per turn. SGLang's `--enable-session-radix-cache` is not tested: it needs SGLang's
  own session API, which this client does not use.

- **H8 (where the cliff is: a model, tested).** Let K be the GPU's KV capacity (244,928 tokens, from vLLM's log) and s
  a session's context (about 10.5k tokens at the median of a 150 s step). Under LRU a session's next turn hits only if
  the other sessions touched since its last turn, plus itself, fit in K. Two models give two predictions:
  (A) sessions come back in a fixed cycle, so all N-1 others are touched: hits vanish above N* = K/s, about 23;
  (B) the order is random, so about (1 - 1/e)(N-1) distinct others are touched: hits vanish above
  N* = 1 + K/(0.63 s), about 38. Run vLLM GPU-only at 24, 32 and 40 sessions (pauses 5 and 30 s, 2 rounds) and fit
  the measured hit rate per resumed turn to both; report which model fits, or that neither does.
  Energy per turn is then predicted from the hit rate as E = e_r x (uncached tokens) + E_decode, with
  e_r = P_busy / (prefill tokens per second), about 0.075 J per token on this card, and compared to the measurement.

- **H9 (offload works only when the host tier holds the working set).** Seen before writing this: SGLang with an 80 GB
  host tier (305,176 tokens, from its log) raised the hit at 48 sessions only from 0.1% to 12.6% and cut energy per
  turn by 10% (H3 not supported). Our reading: the host tier holds a copy of what is on the GPU, so it adds little room
  beyond the GPU's own pool, and 48 sessions (~500k tokens) still do not fit, so the same cliff happens one tier down.
  With a 160 GB host tier (about 610k tokens; SGLang uses pinned host memory, not /dev/shm, and the container allows
  454 GiB), the hit at 48 sessions is at least 50% and energy per turn at least 20% lower than GPU-only, on both
  pauses; at 96 sessions (~1M tokens) the benefit stays under 10%. Exploratory: added after H3's result.

- **H10 (transfer or recompute, measured directly).** The question the position paper 2609.05565 asks and does not
  measure. For 8 prompts of ~4k, ~8k and ~16k tokens sent at once (one output token each, 3 repetitions): above-idle
  GPU energy per prompt token of a restore from SGLang's host tier (160 GB) is at most 1/10 of a full recompute, and
  a GPU prefix-cache hit at most 1/20. A GPU-only engine run through the same steps is the control: after the flush
  its third send must cost the same as a recompute (within 10%) and report no cached tokens. Host CPU and memory energy
  are not metered in this container (see below).

- **H13 (a controller that reads the cliff model live).** Phase 2, vLLM, 48 and 96 sessions, pauses 5 and 30 s,
  2 rounds. The formula controller sets the number of admitted sessions to K = floor(0.9 C / s), with C read from the
  engine and s the 90th-percentile recent prompt plus one turn of growth; sessions yield after 8 turns. Against
  GPU-only it raises the cache hit to at least 50% and lowers GPU energy per turn by at least 30% at both loads. A
  CONCUR-style AIMD window (alpha 2, beta 0.5, hit threshold 0.2, KV-use thresholds 0.2 / 0.5) saves less energy per
  turn than the formula controller in at least 3 of the 4 cells. Resume latency including the wait for a slot is
  reported for all three.

- **H14 (real agents).** Replaying 400 recorded SWE-agent trajectories (nebius/SWE-agent-trajectories, Llama-70B
  runs; median 16 turns, final context ~12k tokens, answers ~90 tokens, all sessions sharing one system prompt) with
  exact recorded prompts, at 16, 48 and 96 sessions, pause 5 s, 2 rounds: (a) the cache cliff appears: hit above 60%
  at 16 sessions, and at 96 sessions it falls to about the shared system-prompt share of the prompt (under 25%);
  (b) at 96 sessions GPU-only energy per turn is within 10% of no cache; (c) the formula controller lowers energy per
  turn by at least 20% against GPU-only at 96 sessions and beats the CONCUR-style AIMD window there; (d) SGLang with a
  160 GB host tier lowers energy per turn by at least 20% at 48 sessions. Pause times are still synthetic (the
  recordings have no timings); eviction in these engines is pressure-driven, not time-driven.

Changes to the plan, and why:

- vLLM offload runs with a 70 GiB host tier, not 80: the container's /dev/shm is 75 GiB and cannot be resized. It
  runs after the main run, two rounds, not interleaved with the other policies. SGLang keeps its 80 GB tier.
- CPU energy cannot be read in this container (RAPL in sysfs and perf events are both blocked). H5 is judged from a
  bound: the engine's own CPU time (from /proc, logged every second) and the bytes moved to and from host memory, at
  pessimistic watts per core and joules per byte. A direct measurement needs a bare-metal host (E6).

## Literature read in full (6 November 2026, during the run)

- **Already known, cite, do not claim:** agent KV thrashing and agent-level admission (CONCUR 2601.22705, H100,
  Qwen3-32B, latency only; MARS 2604.26963; Robot fleets 2611.01404, static cap K, cliff at about C/s); the cliff
  model N_c ~ 1.9 C/c (Metastability 2611.02543, disaggregated SGLang, eviction policy barely helps, goodput only);
  GPU hit vs recompute J/token by regression (Librarian 2605.27787: 0.034 J/token uncached, 27B FP8 on B200); offload
  energy under agentic load (HotCarbon 2026, Mao and Zhou: vLLM, Llama-3.1-8B, H200, saves 14-32% only above about
  90% p95 KV use, host RAPL difference under 1% of the GPU saving); GPU-only telemetry misses 41-45% of system energy
  for agents (2609.29707).
- **Asked for and not measured by anyone:** an explicit transfer-versus-recompute energy model (2609.05565, sec. 3.3,
  RQ2, sec. 10 item 4); a direct decomposition of offload reload energy ("cannot be decomposed post-hoc", HotCarbon);
  cliff model bounds for colocated prefill and decode (2611.02543, sec. 9).
- **What stays ours:** (1) thrashing costs the same GPU energy as no cache (two engines); (2) direct J/token of host
  restore next to GPU hit and recompute (H10); (3) HiCache energy in a deep collapse, and host-tier sizing; (4) energy
  of an admission gate and of LFU/SLRU under thrashing; (5) the cliff model checked in a colocated engine (H8 tells
  factor 1 from about 1.6-1.9). All energy here is GPU board energy and is labeled so.
- **Watch:** HotCarbon saw vLLM throughput collapse with zero preemptions at offload buffers of 64 GiB or more; our
  70 GiB vLLM rerun must be checked for that.

## Rules

Hardware energy counters only; idle measured after the temperature settles; alternating order; every failure
counted; limits stated (one model, one GPU type, synthetic sessions). Scripts are tested end to end on fake engines
before any GPU time is rented.
